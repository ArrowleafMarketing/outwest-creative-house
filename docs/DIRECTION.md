# OutWest — Owner's Creative Direction

**Source:** the owner's notes, drafted with ChatGPT, shared September 2026.
**Status:** directional rather than a signed-off brand document, but **cleared by Ryan
(Arrowleaf) to work from as fact for now** — build on it. Where it conflicts with
[`BRAND.md`](./BRAND.md), the delivered brand guide, see
[Conflicts](#conflicts-to-resolve) below; those still need owner decisions, because the
two documents give genuinely opposite instructions and no amount of good faith resolves
them silently.

> **Reference sites:** [`REFERENCES.md`](./REFERENCES.md) studies the nine inspiration
> sites the designer and owner picked, and pulls out the specific mechanics to build from.

## The core repositioning

> OutWest is not simply a photography studio. It is a creative house — a physical and
> cultural home for independent creatives, brands and businesses to make work that gets
> remembered.

- **OUTWEST** is the brand. **CREATIVE HOUSE** describes the world around it. The
  **yucca** is the symbol of that world.
- The brand should feel *bigger than the building* from day one.
- Photography lives here, but so do brand campaigns, commercial shoots, content
  production, videography, social, product launches, editorial work, creative direction
  and collaboration.
- Long-term goal: **recognition without words** — someone sees the yucca and knows.

**The north star.** A visitor's first thought should not be "this is a photography
studio." It should be *"What is this place?"* → *"I want my brand here."* →
*"I want to be part of this."*

**The emotional progression** the site must produce:
`WHAT IS THIS?` → `I WANT TO BE THERE.` → `I CAN SEE MY BRAND THERE.` → `HOW DO I BOOK IT?`

**The brand test.** Cover the logo. Does it still feel like OutWest — through light,
texture, typography, photography, colour, pacing, people, the yucca? If yes, there's a
visual language rather than a logo and a website.

**The end goal.** Not "that's a nice studio," but "those are my people," "that's where my
brand belongs," "I want to make something there." And above all: they remember it.

## The feeling

> Luxury fashion house × high-desert architecture × independent creative culture.

Amangiri's architectural restraint and relationship with landscape, plus the editorial
confidence of a luxury fashion campaign.

Creative. Editorial. Aspirational. Established. But also warm, tactile, a little
unexpected. **Not pristine to the point of sterile** — texture, movement, sunlight, soul.

Three things must work together:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Architecture** | Space, materials, scale, light |
| **Fashion** | Editorial photography, typography, styling, confidence |
| **Culture** | People, brands, ICONS, collaborations, community |

Architecture without fashion → boutique hotel. Fashion without architecture → clothing
brand. Both without culture → beautiful but empty.

## "Western" the OutWest way

Never overtly Western. No cowboy fonts, rope, horseshoes, traditional Southwestern
graphics, rustic farmhouse, obvious desert branding.

The West shows up through **material, landscape, attitude and light**: worn cognac
leather, faded denim, sun-bleached plaster, desert stone, olive and yucca, weathered
wood, touches of silver and chrome, hard afternoon light, long shadows, open space,
women photographed with confidence and movement.

> Western through a fashion lens, not a theme.

## Texture and light as brand elements

**Texture** is one of the most recognizable devices: plaster, limewash, stone, leather,
wood grain, linen, sand, fabric, weathered surfaces. It must feel *photographed and
tactile* — never a generic digital texture overlay.

**Light** is treated as a graphic element. Not merely bright natural light but
*directional* light: hard sunlight, long shadows, window patterns, yucca and olive
shadows on plaster, golden-hour warmth, light moving across textured walls. Photography
should capture these deliberately, so an image reads as OutWest without the logo.

## Colour

The notes call for a **quiet foundation** so photography can change constantly without
fighting the brand — "bone + black + photography + texture," with earth tones appearing
as *punctuation*, never colour-blocking. That principle matches the approved palette.

The named colours, however, only partly match. See [Conflicts](#conflicts-to-resolve).

| Group | Notes call for | In the approved palette? |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | Warm limestone, bone, sand, soft plaster, cream | ✅ Alabaster / Canvas |
| Depth | Chocolate, espresso, black olive, cognac leather | ⚠️ Only Olive and near-black Ink |
| OutWest earth | Muted terracotta, clay, adobe, dusty rose | ✅ Adobe / Desert Rose |
| Landscape | Yucca, olive, desert sage | ✅ Olive / Agave |
| Unexpected accent | Faded denim blue | ✅ Horizon |
| Material accent | Chrome / aged silver | ❌ Nothing equivalent |

## Typography

Closer to a luxury fashion, beauty or design house than a photography studio.

- **Primary serif** — elegant, modern, architectural. `O U T W E S T`, large statements,
  section openings, editorial titles, ICONS profiles.
- **Clean sans** — very simple, widely tracked. Navigation, `C R E A T I V E  H O U S E`,
  descriptions, pricing, booking, small labels.
- **Editorial italic** — sparingly, for personality. "Just enough to create tension
  between polished and expressive."

Typography should be confident enough that whole sections can be little more than
beautiful type and negative space.

*This maps cleanly onto the installed stack: Abril (`font-display`), Hanken Grotesk
(`font-sans`), Preztik italic (`.paragraph-header`).*

## The yucca mark

> Minimal, stylized. Not botanical. Not Southwestern. **Not illustrated.** Sculptural.
> Architectural. Proprietary. Simplified until it almost becomes a geometric symbol.

Inside a simple oval or architectural frame. Must work embossed into stationery, stamped
on packaging, etched into glass, fabricated in metal, pressed into leather, on
merchandise, mounted to the studio wall, and alone as the social profile image.

⚠️ **This directly contradicts the delivered badge asset and `BRAND.md`.** See
[Conflicts](#conflicts-to-resolve).

| # | Beat | Content |

| 02 | **The statement** | Huge type, deep negative space: **MAKE THEM / REMEMBER YOU.** Then small: the creative-house one-liner. `DISCOVER OUTWEST →` |
| 03 | **The manifesto** | Small `WELCOME OUT WEST`; large `NOT JUST A STUDIO. A PLACE TO MAKE SOMETHING MATTER.` Restrained copy on the creative-house concept. |
| 04 | **The house** | Not three cards — huge imagery, one space per scroll. **THE WAREHOUSE** ("LIGHT. SCALE. POSSIBILITY."), **THE VILLA** ("SOMEWHERE ELSE, WITHOUT LEAVING BOISE."), **THE CREATIVE LOUNGE** ("WHERE THE WORK HAPPENS BETWEEN THE SHOTS."). Each `EXPLORE →`. |
| 05 | **The scale moment** | Enormous `5,000`, small `SQUARE FEET OF ROOM TO CREATE.` One private booking, one creative house, no shared studios. `BOOK THE HOUSE →` |

| 07 | **The ICONS** | Feels like an editorial magazine inside the site; black background works here. Small `AN OUTWEST ORIGINAL`, huge `THE ICONS`. Portraits moving horizontally, `ICON NO. 01…`, each opening an editorial profile. |

| 09 | **Membership** | **THE OUTWEST COLLECTIVE.** Large: `MAKE THIS YOUR CREATIVE HOME BASE.` Tiers: Creator, Brand Builder, Studio Partner. Emotional sell before the math — never a SaaS pricing table. |
| 10 | **Booking** | `YOUR HOUSE FOR THE HOUR. OR THE WHOLE DAY.` Key statement: **EVERY BOOKING IS PRIVATE — THE ENTIRE 5,000 SQ FT IS YOURS.** `CHECK AVAILABILITY →` |

**Navigation (owner's version):** `THE HOUSE · THE ICONS · THE COLLECTIVE · MADE OUT WEST ·
ABOUT · BOOK` — OUTWEST left, BOOK right.

**Spaces are presented as destinations within a hotel or fashion house**, not rentable
rooms. The homepage sells the feeling; the booking pages carry the information.

## Micro-interactions

Expensive, not gimmicky. Images reveal slowly on scroll; type moves slightly against
photography; the yucca appears almost as a watermark; hovering THE HOUSE subtly changes
the background image; editorial photographs gently overlap; texture transitions plaster →
stone → paper; some imagery moves horizontally rather than vertically; occasional type
runs vertically along an image edge like magazine art direction.

**Everything slow and deliberate.** No bouncy animation, no flashy transitions, no trendy
tricks just because they're possible.

## Photography direction

> This may be more important than the actual website design.

**Do not** fill the site with wide-angle photos of empty rooms. A dedicated OutWest brand
campaign should be shot specifically for the website.

Shoot: wide architecture, extreme details, people, fashion, movement, hands, leather,
denim, chrome, yucca, hard light, long shadows, fabric, film grain, blur, golden hour,
black and white, unexpected crops, behind-the-scenes, finished campaign imagery.

**Rhythm:** alternate `PLACE → PERSON → DETAIL → WORK`. That cadence keeps it editorial.

## What we don't want

No photography-studio template. No boho. No Southwestern boutique. No rustic Western. No
beige-everything influencer aesthetic. No camera illustrations, aperture logos, or
equipment icons. No giant homepage FAQ. No endless amenity bullets. No generic smiling
stock. No busy grids of tiny photographs. No excessive rounded buttons. No overly trendy
web effects.

> No site that screams RENT OUR PHOTO STUDIO. OutWest should whisper something much
> stronger: **come make something here.**

## Conflicts to resolve

These are places where the owner's notes and the approved brand guide give **different
instructions**. Each needs an owner decision before it drives code.

### 1. The yucca: hand-drawn or geometric? — *highest impact*

- **`BRAND.md` / delivered assets:** hand-drawn, "charcoal-like texture and imperfect,
  expressive form," a "fine-art sensibility" and "natural counterpoint to the precision of
  the typography." The badge and all nine illustrations in `public/brand/` are drawn this way.
- **Owner's notes:** "Not illustrated. Sculptural. Architectural. Proprietary… simplified
  until it almost becomes a geometric symbol."

These are opposite briefs. The notes' stated applications — embossed, etched into glass,
fabricated in metal, pressed into leather — genuinely favour a geometric mark; fine
charcoal texture degrades badly at those sizes and in those materials.

**Decision needed:** keep the hand-drawn mark, commission a geometric companion for
small/physical use, or replace it. Until then the existing badge stays in place.

### 2. Navigation and section names

| Approved guide | Owner's notes |
|---|---|
| THE STUDIO | THE HOUSE |
| SETS | *(folded into THE HOUSE)* |
| MEMBERSHIP | THE COLLECTIVE |
| JOURNAL | THE ICONS |
| *(none)* | MADE OUT WEST |
| ABOUT | ABOUT |
| INQUIRE | BOOK |

The notes' naming is stronger and consistent with the repositioning; the guide's is what
was signed off. **Pick one before the nav is built** — these become URLs, and changing
them later costs redirects and SEO.

### 3. "The OutWest Edit" vs "THE ICONS"

The guide describes a print + digital publication called **The OutWest Edit**, tied to a
**Journal** section. The notes describe an editorial property called **THE ICONS**. The
Edit's own mock cover shows "ICON No.05," so ICONS may be a *department within* the Edit
rather than a replacement. **Needs clarifying** — it decides whether this is one content
system or two.

### 4. Palette extensions

The notes ask for **chocolate, espresso, cognac** and a **chrome / aged silver** accent.
Nothing in the approved ten-colour palette covers them — Adobe `#865336` is the deepest
warm tone and there is no metallic. Chrome in particular cannot be a flat hex; it has to
be photography, foil, or a gradient treatment, which is a different decision.

**Decision needed:** extend the palette with approved hexes, or treat these as
*photographic* qualities (cognac leather, chrome objects in shot) rather than UI colours.
The second reading is more consistent with "most pages should be beautifully restrained."

### 5. Audience: photographers, or everyone?

The guide's three personas are two photographers and one small-business owner. The notes
say the opposite — "OutWest should never look like it belongs only to photographers" — and
list filmmakers, founders, agencies, creators, artists and brands.

Worth noting: **the guide's own homepage mock already hedged on this.** The laptop in the
mock reads "A HOME FOR PHOTOGRAPHERS" while the browser window behind it reads "A HOME FOR
VISIONARIES" — the same tension, unresolved, inside the approved deck. The owner's notes
are effectively picking the second one.

The guide's own design goal 04 ("Growth ready — expand beyond studio rentals") supports
the broader read. **But the persona research doesn't cover the wider audience yet**, so
copy aimed at founders and agencies would be written without evidence.

### 6. Film on the homepage

The notes open with a full-screen cinematic film. The guide never mentions video. This is
a real scope and performance consideration — a hero video needs a poster frame, a reduced-
motion fallback, a mobile strategy, and the film itself doesn't exist yet.

## Working facts

These appear only in the owner's notes, and are cleared to build with:

- **5,000 square feet.** Used twice as a headline number.
- **Space names:** THE WAREHOUSE, THE VILLA, THE CREATIVE LOUNGE.
- **Membership tiers:** Creator, Brand Builder, Studio Partner.
- **"Every booking is private"** — the whole house, no shared studios.
- **Boise, Idaho** as the stated location *(corroborated by `BRAND.md`)*.

One carve-out worth keeping in mind: "every booking is private" and the square footage are
**commercial claims a customer could hold OutWest to**. Fine for building and for internal
review; worth one confirmation pass with the owner before the site goes live.
